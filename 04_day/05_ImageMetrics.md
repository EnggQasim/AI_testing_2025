The Inception Score (IS) is a popular metric used to evaluate the quality and diversity of images generated by models such as GANs. It leverages a pretrained classifier (often an Inception network) to assess whether each generated image is both:

1. **Classifiable:** The model assigns it a “sharp” or confident label distribution \(p(y|x)\), meaning the image clearly looks like one class.
2. **Diverse:** The overall distribution \(p(y)\) (computed across many generated images) is spread out, indicating that the generator produces a variety of images.

The Inception Score is defined as:

\[
\text{IS} = \exp\left( \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_g}\left[ \text{KL}\left(p(y|x) \,||\, p(y)\right) \right] \right)
\]

where:
- \(p(y|x)\) is the conditional label distribution for an image \(x\),
- \(p(y)\) is the marginal distribution, computed as the average of \(p(y|x)\) over all generated images, and
- \(\text{KL}(\cdot||\cdot)\) is the Kullback-Leibler divergence.

Below is a simple example illustrating the calculation:

---

### Numerical Example

Imagine you have three generated images. For simplicity, suppose our classifier predicts probabilities over just two classes. Here are the predictions:

- **Image 1:** \( p(y|x_1) = [0.9, 0.1] \)
- **Image 2:** \( p(y|x_2) = [0.8, 0.2] \)
- **Image 3:** \( p(y|x_3) = [0.7, 0.3] \)

**Step 1: Compute the Marginal Distribution \(p(y)\)**
\[
p(y) = \frac{1}{3} \Big( [0.9, 0.1] + [0.8, 0.2] + [0.7, 0.3] \Big) = [0.8, 0.2]
\]

**Step 2: Compute the KL Divergence for Each Image**

For an individual image, the KL divergence is calculated as:
\[
\text{KL}(p(y|x) \parallel p(y)) = \sum_{i} p(y=i|x) \log \frac{p(y=i|x)}{p(y=i)}
\]

- **Image 1:**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{KL}_1 &= 0.9 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.9}{0.8}\right) + 0.1 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.1}{0.2}\right) \\
  &\approx 0.9 \cdot 0.1178 + 0.1 \cdot (-0.6931) \\
  &\approx 0.1060 - 0.0693 = 0.0367
  \end{aligned}
  \]

- **Image 2:**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{KL}_2 &= 0.8 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.8}{0.8}\right) + 0.2 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.2}{0.2}\right) \\
  &= 0.8 \cdot 0 + 0.2 \cdot 0 = 0
  \end{aligned}
  \]

- **Image 3:**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{KL}_3 &= 0.7 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.7}{0.8}\right) + 0.3 \cdot \log\left(\frac{0.3}{0.2}\right) \\
  &\approx 0.7 \cdot (-0.1335) + 0.3 \cdot 0.4055 \\
  &\approx -0.0935 + 0.1217 = 0.0282
  \end{aligned}
  \]

**Step 3: Average the KL Divergence and Exponentiate**

\[
\text{Average KL} = \frac{0.0367 + 0 + 0.0282}{3} \approx 0.0216
\]
\[
\text{IS} = \exp(0.0216) \approx 1.022
\]

A higher Inception Score indicates both high-quality (confident) and diverse images. In our simple example, an IS of approximately 1.022 suggests the images are not very diverse or confident overall, which is often the case in toy examples.

### Python Code Example

Here's a short Python snippet that performs the above calculation:

```python
import numpy as np

# Predicted class probabilities for three images (each row corresponds to an image)
probs = np.array([
    [0.9, 0.1],
    [0.8, 0.2],
    [0.7, 0.3]
])

# Step 1: Compute the marginal probability p(y)
p_y = np.mean(probs, axis=0)
print("Marginal p(y):", p_y)

# Step 2: Compute the KL divergence for each image
kl_divs = np.sum(probs * np.log(probs / p_y), axis=1)
print("KL divergences for each image:", kl_divs)

# Step 3: Compute average KL divergence and then the Inception Score
avg_kl = np.mean(kl_divs)
inception_score = np.exp(avg_kl)
print("Average KL divergence:", avg_kl)
print("Inception Score:", inception_score)
```

This code calculates the marginal distribution, computes the KL divergence for each image, averages these divergences, and finally exponentiates the result to yield the Inception Score.
